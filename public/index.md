# No Zabiha Halal. The stamp is not a standard.

Halal is not a nutrition label. It is a religious permission slip.

Halal does not inform. It rules.

## 01 — Opening

Halal means permitted under Islamic law. Zabiha is the slaughter strict certifiers demand before they will call the meat halal. Without that rite, the same authorities call ordinary American meat haram. Not unsafe. Not uninspected. Not nutritionally different. Forbidden.

A republic does not take food orders from a forbidden-and-permitted stamp. This page is the commercial half of the refusal. The cut itself is answered at https://nozabiha.com/.

The page quotes a grocery warning and answers it: meat that is not slaughtered according to zabiha is typically deemed non-halal, including meat from conventional grocery stores. The page says that paragraph describes a doctrine. It is not a fact about American meat.

## What the two words are doing

Zabiha is the cut. Halal is the verdict. Sellers swap them when it helps the sale, and split them when a stunned bird still needs a sticker.

### The verdict

Their claim: halal means the food is lawful, and non-zabiha is cast as generally unlawful. The refusal: lawful for a doctrine is not lawful-as-a-fact. Once a package uses "halal" the way other packages use "Grade A," a religious court has been slipped into the meat case.

### The cut behind the sticker

Their claim: without zabiha, meat is generally not permissible. The refusal: the sticker is not a spice blend or a cleanliness claim. It is a claim that the animal was killed under Islamic rules. Ordinary inspected meat is not illicit because a certifier frowned at it.

### The internal split they hide

Their claim: one Muslim rule, one certified answer, no daylight. The refusal: Quran 5:5 calls the food of Jews and Christians lawful. A large share of Muslims in the West eat supermarket meat on that basis. Zabiha agencies exist to close that door and sell the stricter one. A divided theology is being marketed as a single consumer fact.

### The fee

Their claim: certification is just assurance, the way any audit is assurance. The refusal: packers pay a religious agency to watch a line and issue a mark. That mark is not a federal grade. The cost lands in separate SKUs, separate kitchens, and separate bids. Nobody voted for a second food code.

## The product claim, refused

- Halal status. They claim zabiha meat is halal and non-zabiha is generally haram. The refusal: a binary religious grade does not belong on a civic meat supply. Inspected is the public word. Permitted-by-fiqh is a private one.
- Slaughter method. They claim Islamic rites versus methods that may not follow Islamic guidelines. The refusal: guidelines of a religion are not a defect report. A plant can miss Islamic guidelines and still meet the only code this country enacted.
- Invocation. They claim God's name is required, and usually missing on ordinary meat. The refusal: then the missing item is a prayer, not a safety step. Do not print a prayer-gap as if it were a recall.
- Permissibility. They claim meat is permissible for Muslims only when certified. The refusal: shoppers who are not under that law are not a compliance problem. Segregating the whole case to soothe the strict reading is the compliance problem.

## Procurement is the back door

A district, a hospital, a jail, or a city agency drops pork, then drops non-halal vendors, then calls the remainder inclusive. The page says a single halal menu is a rule, not hospitality. The child, patient, or inmate who is not Muslim did not enroll in a dietary law.

The Humane Methods of Slaughter Act, 7 U.S.C. § 1902, lets the rite enter American law as an exemption from stunning for religious slaughter, not as the standard. The page says halal certification is the market version of the same move: first the carve-out, then the logo, then the contract that will only buy the logo.

The physical cut is answered on nozabiha.com. Short version from this page: stunning is the Western welfare baseline; a conscious throat-cut is the thing the exemption permits. A gold-green stamp does not change the physiology.

## Conventional grocery meat is not contraband

Temperature abuse, pathogens, and drug residues are public hazards. A slaughterman failing to pronounce a religious formula is not. The page rejects treating Kroger, Costco, and the corner market as presumptively unlawful meat.

## Who the sticker is actually for

If halal were only a voluntary mark, the page would be shorter. The growth market it names is airlines, campuses, hospitals, stadiums, and school bids, where one certified line is cheaper than two and the uncertified line disappears. Non-Muslim customers then pay for a rite they did not ask for.

The page points to islamophobe.me for the broader legal case and industrialterrorism.com as a sister record. This site stays on the food.

## The line in the sand

A halal requirement in public institutions is a Muslim practice wearing a procurement form. The page says it is not compatible with Western American values.

One civil food law. Equal service. Animal welfare set by veterinarians and by statute, not by fiqh. No religious test on a lunch tray a child cannot opt out of.

Private houses, private grocers, and private certification for private buyers can continue. The moment the stamp becomes the condition of a public meal, the page says the practice has left opinion and entered rule. A person is not the target. The jurisdiction is.

## What refusal looks like

- Speak plainly. A religious cut is not a nutrition fact, and "halal" is not a USDA grade.
- Hold the public table. Refuse halal-only certification as the default menu in schools, hospitals, prisons, and city contracts.
- Stunning is the standard. Back animal-welfare rules that require insensibility before the cut, including on meat bought with public money.
- Pass the page on. Send it to the school board, the procurement office, and anyone still treating the sticker as neutral.

Leave the stamp on the private shelf. Keep it off the public menu.
